05 · A Liga F xG model

Is StatsBomb's model wrong, or just mis-scaled?

I trained an xG model on this season's shots, using location, body part, technique, assist type and freeze-frame features such as how many defenders blocked the goal. Every model is scored only on matches it never saw. The chart compares predicted and actual scoring rates; a perfectly calibrated model sits on the dotted line.

06 · What makes a shot go in

What drives the Liga F model?

These are standardised effects from the logistic version of the model. Blue features raise the chance of scoring and red ones lower it, holding everything else constant. Distance and angle dominate, as in any xG model, but the freeze-frame features add real information: the number of defenders in the shooting lane matters almost as much as whether the shot is a header.

08 · Team styles

Which teams look similar?

Principal component analysis (PCA) condenses seven standardised attacking, progression and defensive measures into a two-dimensional map. Colours are K-means groups fitted on the same seven measures. They describe similar profiles, not fixed tactical labels.

How to read the map

What the two axes measure.

All seven measures are standardised before fitting, so a high-volume measure such as completed passes cannot dominate just because of its scale. Axis directions are fixed so that moving right always means more points per match.

What the colours mean

The groups are performance levels, not attacking identities.

09 · Passing style

Progression is more than possession.

This compares completed passing volume with open-play progressive passes, meaning completed passes that move the ball at least 10 yards closer to the opponent's goal. Restarts such as goal kicks are excluded from progression.

10 · Passing network

Who connects with whom?

This shows each team's eleven most frequent starters across the season. Dots sit at each player's average position when passing or receiving, and lines show pairs who completed at least ten passes to each other. Thicker lines mean more passes.

11 · Defensive context

What do teams allow, and where do they win the ball back?

High regains are successful ball recoveries and interceptions in the attacking third. Lower xG conceded means a team allowed fewer and worse chances. The best defensive profiles sit in the bottom right.

13 · Expected threat

Every pass and carry counts.

Derived xA only rewards the last pass before a shot. Expected threat (xT) values every area of the pitch by how likely possession there is to become a goal, so any completed pass or carry that moves the ball somewhere more dangerous earns credit. The map shows those values, fitted on this season's open play.
